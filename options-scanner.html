Options Scanner, by Zelos.

Options Scanner doubles as an add-on to Swing Trader and Breakout Rider: each session it first checks whether either one already published today's headline stock pick, and if that setup also clears the options-specific checks, it becomes today's options idea instead of a separate call. If neither is running for you, or neither found anything today, it runs the same directional screen on its own — pullbacks holding in an uptrend, breakdowns failing to hold support — then translates whatever clears the bar into one specific options contract: a single long call or put, roughly one strike out of the money, 30–45 days to expiration. Never spreads, never multi-leg, never short options, never 0DTE, never weeklies. It's not tied to your account and never places an order — what you do with the information is entirely up to you.

What it's actually looking for

Two directions. One contract rule. Nothing else counts.

Every candidate gets sorted into one of these — or thrown out. The direction decides call or put; the contract rule never changes.

Selling pressure fades, support holds, the trend resumes — the setup a long call gets built from.

Breakdown that fails to hold

↓ long put

How it decides

Four checks, every session, before anything gets suggested.

01

Read the market

Checks whether stocks broadly are trending up, down, or sideways first — the bar for a good setup gets stricter when the backdrop is weak.

02

Check for a same-day pick first, then scan on its own

If you also run Swing Trader or Breakout Rider, it checks whether either one already published a real headline setup today and uses that as the candidate — so this can feel like the options way to play today's call rather than an unrelated scan. Otherwise, it screens hundreds of stocks itself for the same pullback-holding or breakdown-failing shapes those two look for — scored for a clean directional move, not a stock pick on its own. Either way, the candidate still has to clear every check below; being linked never skips a step.

03

Translate it to one contract

Once a setup clears the bar, it's translated into exactly one options structure: call or put based on direction, roughly one strike out of the money, 30–45 days to expiration — enough runway for the move to play out without a weekly option's time decay eating it alive. Never a spread, never multiple legs, never a short option.

04

You decide, every time

If something clears the bar, the alert publishes the underlying setup plus the contract detail — direction, approximate strike, expiration window — and a general risk-sizing note, never a number calculated from your account, since Zelos never sees it. What you do with that information, in your own brokerage account, is entirely your call.

Trust & safety

The rules that don't bend.

✓

No account connection, ever. Zelos never asks for your brokerage credentials and never links to any account. There's nothing to authorize and nothing for it to touch — it publishes an alert page, full stop.

✓

Nothing here places an order. Every run only researches and reports. If you decide to act on an alert, you place that options trade yourself, in your own brokerage account, on your own judgment.

✓

Long calls or long puts only — never spreads, multi-leg, short options, 0DTE, or weeklies. This is the rule that doesn't bend. A single long option has a plainly defined maximum loss — what you paid for it — and nothing more complicated to manage or get assigned on.

✓

Sizing is a general rule of thumb, not a calculation on your money. Because Zelos never sees your balance, it can't and doesn't size a position for you. Options can expire worthless, so the general guidance here leans smaller than the stock-alert equivalent — apply it to your own situation.

✓

"No trade today" is a normal outcome. The bar exists to be missed most days — that's what keeps it a strategy instead of a habit.

✓

Nothing here is a guarantee. This approach hasn't been backtested against a long track record. It's rules, applied consistently — not a promise of returns.

Does it work with Swing Trader or Breakout Rider?

Yes — that's what it's built for. If you also run either one, Options Scanner checks each session whether that skill already published a real headline setup for you today, and if it clears the same options-specific checks (a liquid options chain, the qualifying score), that becomes today's options idea automatically. If neither is running for you, or neither found a setup that day, it runs its own independent bullish/bearish scan instead. Either path still has to pass the full screen — nothing gets a pass just because it's linked.

Why calls and puts only — never spreads?

It keeps the position simple and the risk plainly defined: the most you can lose is what you paid for the contract, never more, and there's only one leg to track — no multi-leg management, no assignment risk from a short leg you sold. That's a deliberate trade-off against the extra income or defined-risk shapes a spread can offer.

What's the strike and expiration rule?

Roughly one strike out of the money, 30–45 days to expiration. That's enough runway for the underlying move to actually play out without a weekly option's fast time decay working against the position before the setup has a chance to be right.
